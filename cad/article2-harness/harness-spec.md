# Article 2 — wiring harness and programming table

Flight article 2. Successor to hb1 (article 1, MATEKF722MINI). The change that drives this
document: the receiver is now **inside** the flight controller, so article 1's external receiver is
gone and with it the servo power rail that hung off it. Everything downstream has to be re-derived.

Status: **proposed, nothing built or soldered.** Items marked ⚠️ are unverified and must be checked
against hardware before they become solder joints.

---

## 1. Parts

| Ref | Part | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | HGLRC Specter F722 Lite | STM32F722, ICM42688P, DPS310, 16 MB flash, onboard ELRS 2.4 G, 5 V 2 A BEC, 2–6S. Pads on **both faces**, so the symbol has two units |
| U2 | Seeed XIAO nRF52840 | the policy MCU, MSP link to U1 on UART1 |
| U3 | Matek M10Q-5883 | GNSS + QMC5883L compass, 4–9 V, 13 mA |
| U4 | ~40 A BLHeli ESC with SBEC | three units, one per real connector: J_PACK, J_CTRL, J_PHASE |
| MOT1 | BLDC outrunner | same motor as article 1. Refdes is MOT1, not M1 — M1 is an FC output pad |
| S1, S2 | Goteck GS-5010MG | 1.4 kg·cm @ 6 V metal-gear micro, 4.8–6 V |
| BT1 | 3S LiPo | XT60 |

Symbols are in `article2-harness.kicad_sym`, generated by `gen_symbols.py`; pin-map provenance is in
that script's header. Do not hand-edit the `.kicad_sym` or the `.kicad_sch` — change the generator
and run `./check.sh`.

**U1 is split by board face.** Unit 1 is the TOP face: UART1, UART3, the ELRS breakout cluster,
CAM/VTX, LED, BB-, VIN. Unit 2 is the BOTTOM face: the 4-in-1 pad row (BAT, GND, M1–M4, CR, RX6)
and the GPS row (5V, GND, TX4, RX4, SDA, SCL). Knowing which face a pad is on is the difference
between a ten-minute solder job and unmounting the board.

**U4 is split by connector.** J_PACK is the heavy pack leads. J_CTRL is the servo-style lead
carrying throttle in and the BEC out. J_PHASE is the three motor wires. `BAT-` and `BEC_GND` are
separate pins because they are separate wires — they are common only *inside* the ESC, and that
internal bond is the harness star ground.

## 2. Power

Three independent supplies. **Do not tie the 5 V rails together.**

| Rail | Source | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| VBAT | BT1 direct | U4 J_PACK, and U1 BAT_B (the bypass wire) |
| 5V_XIAO / 5V_GPS | U1 onboard BEC, 2 A | U1 itself, onboard ELRS, U2 xiao, U3 GPS |
| 5V_BEC | U4 SBEC, ~3 A | S1 and S2 only |

The two FC-sourced 5 V nets are **named separately on purpose**. They are one rail inside the FC but
two physical wires leaving two different pads on two different faces: `5V_T1` on the top-face left
edge beside RX1/TX1 for the xiao, and `5V_B` in the bottom-face GPS row for the GPS. Same for their
grounds. Naming them apart makes the drawing tell you which pad to solder.

**The bypass wire.** U1's `BAT_B` pad feeds both the onboard regulator and the vbat divider
(ADC channel 1 on PC2). Running raw pack voltage to it is what gives battery telemetry. It must see
the **pack**, not the ESC BEC. On a stacked 4-in-1 this arrives over the ribbon; with a standalone
ESC it is a wire you add.

**Why the servos are on the ESC BEC.** Article 1 powered its servos from the BEC through the
receiver's servo rail, so servo current never touched the FC's 5 V. Article 2 loses that rail, so
feeding the servos from the ESC BEC directly *preserves* article 1's topology rather than changing
it. See §2a for the ground half of that argument, which matters more than the current headroom.

⚠️ The GS-5010MG stall current is not published. For a 1.4 kg·cm metal-gear micro, budget roughly
0.7–1.0 A stalled each. An estimate, not a datasheet figure.

## 2a. Ground topology

**The star point is the ESC's pack-negative pad.** Every return in the aircraft ends there, and the
only ground bonds are inside modules.

Four distinct ground wires:

| Net | Run | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `GND_PACK` | BT1 − → U4 `BAT- (60A)`, and U1 `GND_B` taps the same pads | motor return, 0–40 A |
| `GND_BEC` | U4 `BEC_GND` → S1 GND, S2 GND | servo return, ~2 A peak |
| `GND_XIAO` | U1 `GND_T1` → U2 GND | xiao supply return, ~50 mA |
| `GND_GPS` | U1 `GND_B2` → U3 GND | GPS supply return, 13 mA |

`GND_BEC` bonds to `GND_PACK` **inside U4**. `GND_XIAO` and `GND_GPS` bond to `GND_PACK` **inside
U1**. No ground is bonded twice, so there is no loop.

**Why it is arranged this way.** The FC's ground is the gyro's voltage reference. Any current
flowing through it develops an IR drop that moves that reference, and the gyro is the one sensor
whose noise floor the control loop cannot filter away. Servo current is the largest non-motor
transient in the aircraft and it is switched at the 20 Hz the policy commands, right in the band the
loop cares about. So the design rule is: **servo return current must never share copper with the
FC's ground reference.** Routing it to the ESC BEC and home through the ESC achieves that.

Two mistakes that would undo it:

1. ⚠️ **Do not take servo V+ and GND from the FC.** A three-pin servo header wired to FC pads is the
   obvious, conventional thing to do, and it is wrong here. It puts the full servo return through the
   gyro's reference.
2. ⚠️ **Do not land the ESC lead's `BEC_GND` on an FC pad.** Split the ESC's servo-style lead: the
   signal wire goes to U1 `M3`, the `BEC+5V` and `BEC_GND` wires go to the servo bus. Landing
   `BEC_GND` on the FC both injects servo current into FC ground and creates a second path to the
   star, which is a loop around the motor phase wires.

**What about the throttle signal's return?** It goes FC ground → star → inside the ESC → the ESC's
signal ground. That is a short path because both ground wires land on the same pads. A dedicated
signal-ground wire would shorten it slightly and cost a ground loop; at a 400 Hz PWM with slow edges
that trade is not worth taking.

**What about servo signal thresholds?** The servos reference their PWM input to `GND_BEC`, while the
FC drives it referenced to `GND_PACK`. The offset between them is the servo current times the
resistance of the BEC ground wire through the ESC — tens of millivolts for short, heavy wire. Servo
input thresholds have volts of margin, so this is not a concern. It *would* become one if the FC's
ground spoke were thin or long, so **keep U1's ground wire short and heavy**, landed on the ESC pack
pads.

## 3. Jumpers on the FC board

| Jumper | Setting for article 2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ELRS short-connect pad | **BRIDGE** | enables the internal ELRS receiver on UART2. The manual says to leave it open only when using an external receiver on the RX2/TX2 pads |
| VIN source select | **BAT** (or leave open) | selects whether the VIN pad carries pack voltage or 5 V. Only matters if a VTX or camera is fitted on the top-face left-edge pads. Nothing in article 2 uses it |

## 4. UART map

UART5 is in the INAV target but has no pads. UART6 has an RX6 pad and no TX pad, so it cannot do
duplex. That leaves three usable full-duplex ports, and UART2 is spoken for by the internal receiver.

| Port | CLI index | Pads | Face | Article 2 function | Mask |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VCP (USB) | 20 | USB-C | bottom | MSP | 1 |
| UART1 | 0 | TX1 / RX1 | **top** | **MSP → xiao** | 1 |
| UART2 | 1 | internal, via jumper | — | **Serial RX, CRSF** | 64 |
| UART3 | 2 | TX3 / RX3 | top | spare (telemetry or VTX control) | 0 |
| UART4 | 3 | TX4 / RX4 | **bottom** | **GPS** | 2 |
| UART5 | — | none | — | unusable | — |
| UART6 | 5 | RX6 only | bottom | unusable for duplex | 0 |

**Why the GPS gets UART4.** The bottom face exposes `5V GND TX4 RX4 SDA SCL` as six adjacent pads, a
1:1 match to the M10Q-5883's six-wire connector. One run, no crossing. Article 1 had the GPS on
UART1 and the xiao on UART4; swapping them costs one config line and buys a much cleaner harness.
It also puts the xiao on the opposite face from the GPS, which keeps the two runs from competing.

## 5. Output map

Outputs sharing a timer share a rate, so the servos and the ESC must be on different timers.
M1/M2 are TIM8, M3/M4 are TIM1, which splits exactly the way we need. All four are on the **bottom**
face.

| Pad | MCU pin | Timer | Role | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | PC8 | TIM8 CH3 | S1 servo | 50 Hz |
| M2 | PC9 | TIM8 CH4 | S2 servo | 50 Hz |
| M3 | PA8 | TIM1 CH1 | ESC throttle | 400 Hz (STANDARD) |
| M4 | PA9 | TIM1 CH2 | unused | — |

S5–S8 exist in the INAV target but have **no pads on this board**. The configurator will show eight
outputs; only four are real.

**The GPS connector.** Confirmed on the module: the **forward** connector, the one ahead of the
front-facing arrow, read left to right, is

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| red 5V | yellow RX | blue TX | green CL | white DA | black GND |

Note **RX comes before TX**. An earlier draft of this document had them the other way round, which
would have left the GPS silent and the cause non-obvious.

**The servo bus** is a pair of 3-pin headers in the usual signal / V+ / GND order, with V+ and GND
coming from the ESC BEC and only the signal from the FC.

## 7. INAV CLI block

Every setting name was checked against the autoc fork's `settings.yaml`. Betaflight names like
`mag_bustype` and `mag_i2c_device` do **not** exist in INAV; the compass bus comes from the target.

```
# --- serial ports -------------------------------------------------
serial 20 1 115200 115200 0 115200    # VCP  : MSP over USB
serial 0 1 115200 115200 0 115200     # UART1: MSP -> xiao
serial 1 64 115200 115200 0 115200    # UART2: internal ELRS, CRSF
serial 2 0 115200 115200 0 115200     # UART3: spare
serial 3 2 115200 115200 0 115200     # UART4: GPS
serial 5 0 115200 115200 0 115200     # UART6: unused (RX pad only)

# --- receiver -----------------------------------------------------
set receiver_type = SERIAL
set serialrx_provider = CRSF

# --- outputs ------------------------------------------------------
timer_output_mode 7 SERVOS            # TIM8 -> M1, M2
timer_output_mode 0 MOTORS            # TIM1 -> M3, M4
set servo_pwm_rate = 50
set motor_pwm_protocol = STANDARD

# --- battery ------------------------------------------------------
feature VBAT
set vbat_meter_type = ADC
set vbat_adc_channel = 1
set vbat_scale = 1100                 # trim against a meter; article 1 landed on 1105
set current_meter_type = NONE         # the CR pad wants a 4-in-1 ESC signal we do not have

# --- GPS / compass ------------------------------------------------
set gps_provider = UBLOX
set gps_sbas_mode = AUTO
set mag_hardware = QMC5883

# --- autoc --------------------------------------------------------
set msp_override_channels = 47

save
```

`motor_pwm_rate` is deliberately absent. INAV only consults it when the protocol is BRUSHED; with
STANDARD the ESC rate is hardcoded to 400 Hz, which is why article 1 could carry a nonsense 16000
and fly anyway.

## 8. Verify before soldering

1. ⚠️ **Gyro alignment.** The INAV target inherits `CW0_DEG`; it has not been measured on our
   airframe. Article 1 already cost us a board-alignment bias, so confirm before flight.
2. ⚠️ **vbat scale.** 1100 is the target default. Trim against a meter.
3. ⚠️ **xiao 5 V is USB VBUS**, wired straight through on the XIAO nRF52840. Powering it from the FC
   rail with a powered USB cable attached backfeeds the host port. Known to mostly work; the symptom
   when it does not is the port current-limiting and dropping the console. A data-only cable removes
   it. Worth remembering during the bench failsafe and servo-actuation gates, where a power dropout
   reads like a control-handoff bug.
4. **Ground discipline.** Re-read §2a before wiring the servo bus. Both named mistakes look like the
   conventional thing to do.
5. **Do not bridge 5V_XIAO/5V_GPS to 5V_BEC.** Two regulators in parallel.
6. Four output pads only. Do not plan a fifth channel.
7. Check which **face** each pad is on before planning the physical layout. U1's two units are not
   decoration.
